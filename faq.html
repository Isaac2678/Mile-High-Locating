Private Utility Locating FAQ

Straight answers for contractors and homeowners. Don't see your question? Call (720) 514-2876.

For Contractors & Excavators

When you call 811, Colorado's one-call center notifies member utility companies — electric, gas and telecom providers — to mark their lines. Those marks cover only the lines those companies own, typically ending at the meter or property boundary. Everything past that — secondary electric to a shop, private gas from the meter or a propane tank, site lighting, private lines between buildings — is private infrastructure that 811 won't mark. A private locate finds and marks those lines. More detail: 811 vs. private utility locating.

Yes. A private locate doesn't replace your 811 ticket. Colorado law requires excavators to notify 811 before digging so the member utilities can mark their lines. We cover the private side of the site.

Colorado's Underground Damage Prevention Law (C.R.S. 9-1.5) requires excavators to exercise reasonable care to avoid damaging underground facilities. The law doesn't mandate a private locate by name, but it does require reasonable precautions. Hiring a private locator creates a documentation trail showing you did your due diligence — which matters in any dispute after a strike.

For Homeowners

811 marks lines that utility companies own — the infrastructure that runs up to your meter or property entry point. It doesn't mark anything on your side of that boundary. So if you have a line running to a detached garage, a private gas or propane line, irrigation, or any other privately installed utility, 811 has no record of it and won't mark it. Those are exactly the lines homeowners most often hit.

Any project that disturbs the ground on your property. Common ones include fence installation, landscaping and grading, a patio or deck with footings, drainage work, planting large trees, and adding outbuildings or additions. If a shovel, auger or excavator is going into the ground, get a locate first.
